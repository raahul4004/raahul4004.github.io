SRC-Net — cross-generator detection of AI-generated images

Amazon · 2026

Detectors for synthetic imagery tend to memorise one generator's artifacts and degrade sharply on an unfamiliar source. I set out to measure how much of the reported performance was real.

Part of the answer was uncomfortable. Preprocessing differences between real and generated images — compression, crop, resolution — were doing some of the work models were being credited for. I built benchmarks that control for those confounds, re-evaluated the field's baselines against them, then trained SRC-Net: a frozen CLIP backbone adapted by parameter-efficient fine-tuning, so the pretrained representation stays intact.

LLM inference engine, from scratch

open source

A full autoregressive transformer decoder written from the original papers with no reference implementation: attention, feed-forward blocks, rotary positional embeddings, the sampling loop.

The interesting part was making generation fast. A KV-cache means restructuring the whole decode path around what can be reused between tokens, and that constraint is where most of the learning was.

open source

A retrieval pipeline over corpora of 500+ pages, pairing dense-embedding search with a generative model so answers stay grounded in retrieved passages instead of invented.

Most of the gain came from ablations rather than a better model. Chunk size and embedding strategy moved retrieval quality and latency more than anything else I tried.

Real-time violence detection in video

open source

A 3D convolutional network classifying continuous surveillance video, modelling motion across frames rather than judging stills independently. Frame extraction, temporal windowing, augmentation, batched GPU inference.

It held up across varied lighting, camera angle, and partial occlusion, which is where single-frame approaches tend to break.
